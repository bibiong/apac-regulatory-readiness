Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA)

In force Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas and Department of Justice Scam & fraud

Passed 20 Jul 2024

Obligations

Criminalises money-mule activity, social-engineering schemes and economic sabotage, with coordination duties across financial institutions, law enforcement and platforms.
general · legal, ops, policy
Evidence: Referral and coordination records
BSP-supervised institutions must run real-time automated fraud monitoring, including velocity checks, geolocation and device-change monitoring and blacklist screening, and move away from SMS and email OTPs for high-risk transactions.
BSP Circular 1213 · security, ops
Clock: Compliance deadline 30 June 2026
Evidence: Not a platform duty. Recorded because it sets the counterparty's control expectations in any joint anti-scam workstream.
Platform obligations arise mainly through coordination duties rather than direct designation. Monitored rather than actively managed.

Deliberately the thinnest entry in the tracker, carried at monitor posture — see docs/METHODOLOGY.md on why coverage claims are scoped. Re-verified 2026-10-13: the instrument is now properly identified as RA 12010, signed July 2024, operationalised through BSP circulars with a 30 June 2026 institutional deadline. Confidence raised to medium on the strength of the primary citation. The substantive duties still sit on financial institutions rather than platforms, so the monitor posture is unchanged — this is a counterparty obligation, not a platform one.
