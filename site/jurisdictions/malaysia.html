Online Safety Act 2025

In force Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission Content takedown Age assurance

Commenced 01 Jan 2026

Obligations

Licensed providers must act on harmful content within short statutory response deadlines and maintain user reporting and appeal mechanisms.
general · ops, legal, policy
Clock: Short statutory deadlines set by the Act and MCMC directions
Evidence: Report-to-action timestamps; appeal outcomes
Comply with the Risk Mitigation Code issued under the Act, covering systemic duties to assess and mitigate harmful-content risk.
Risk Mitigation Code · policy, product, ops
Evidence: Risk assessments and mitigation records
Comply with the Child Protection Code issued under the Act, covering minor-specific safeguards.
Child Protection Code · policy, product, engineering
Evidence: Child-safety measure documentation

Penalty exposure

Up to RM 500,000

Fixed sum, with imprisonment up to 5 years available for certain offences

Personal liability in scope.

Licence conditions or withdrawal

Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp were reported among providers deemed licensed from 1 January 2026 under s.46A CMA 1998 deeming provisions, on the 8m+ user threshold.

Licensing is the structural point. A licence converts a foreign service into a regulated entity with standing reporting duties and a licence that can be conditioned or withdrawn — moving the relationship from episodic (they write, you answer) to continuous (you report, they supervise). Continuous supervision is what makes a readiness programme necessary rather than a response queue. Re-verified 2026-10-13: the initial entry missed the two subsidiary codes — the Risk Mitigation Code and the Child Protection Code — which took effect 1 June 2026 and carry the substantive systemic duties.
