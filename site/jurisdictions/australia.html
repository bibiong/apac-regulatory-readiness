Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024

In force Office of the eSafety Commissioner Age assurance

Passed 10 Dec 2024 · Commenced 10 Dec 2025

Obligations

Age-restricted social media platforms must take reasonable steps to prevent Australians under 16 from holding an account.
s.63C · product, engineering, policy, ops
Evidence: Evidence of the steps taken and their effectiveness — age assurance method, detection rates, removal volumes, appeal outcomes. "Reasonable steps" is an effort standard, so the evidence trail is itself the deliverable.
Respond to compulsory information-gathering notices issued by the Commissioner in relation to minimum-age compliance.
general · legal, policy, data, ops
Clock: Set in each notice; non-negotiable
Evidence: Response file, source data lineage, internal approvals

Penalty exposure

Up to AUD 49.5 million

Civil penalty for systemic non-compliance

Public naming in compliance reporting; enforceable undertakings

Enforcement signals

01 Mar 2026 — eSafety published a Social Media Minimum Age compliance update reporting continued under-16 account retention and creation at the three-month mark. [source]

01 Mar 2026 — Five platforms — Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok and YouTube — flagged for compliance concerns; eSafety states it is moving into an enforcement stance. [source]

Facebook and Instagram are named among the five platforms under investigation for suspected non-compliance.

This is the worked example used throughout the readiness framework. A compulsory notice under live investigation is the exact artefact the JD calls a "statutory information request" — fixed deadline, legally compelled, evidence must be defensible if published.
